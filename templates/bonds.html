{# ---------------- Bayesian view ---------------- #}

Bayesian view — posterior for next month's excess return over cash

Prior = the ETF's historical share of months beating cash. Evidence = ten features (trend, momentum, carry, credit spread level/change, curve, real yield, equity trend, VIX) entering a Bayesian ridge regression whose Normal prior shrinks every effect toward zero. Posterior = the updated probability and a 90% credible interval for next month's excess return. Notice how wide the intervals are compared with the means: that width is the honest answer. Out-of-sample check: AUC 0.51, average rank-IC −0.04 — the posterior did not predict realised returns. Treat as a map of uncertainty, not a forecast.

Reading it: the filter lowers max drawdown for every ETF (often by half) and its Sharpe is similar to buy-and-hold (modestly above for Treasuries, TIPS and Agg; slightly below for HYG, VCSH and EMB) — but it never clears the random-timing 95th percentile (right column; green = it does), so the benefit is drawdown control, not proven alpha. Monthly data understates intra-month drawdowns. Bond ETFs barely beat cash after 2011, so buy-and-hold Sharpe ratios are low. Also tested and rejected: trading directly on the Bayesian probability (never reliably better than random timing; hurt junk and EM), and a yield-level/valuation tilt (failed in the 2022 rate shock).
